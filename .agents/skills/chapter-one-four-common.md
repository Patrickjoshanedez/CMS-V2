# Shared Academic and ISO Boundaries: Chapters 1–4

Read this guide with the selected chapter skill. It is supporting guidance, not an
institutional thesis template, ISO standard, or certification scheme.

## Scope, evidence, and revision contract

1. Identify the requested chapter, proposal versus final manuscript, approved headings,
   objectives, methods, source material, and intended output. Read surrounding sections
   only as needed to check consistency. A skill-creation request does not authorize editing
   the live manuscript. A chapter edit authorizes only that chapter unless otherwise stated.
2. Inventory sources and classify claims as observed/measured, reported aggregate,
   documented design, proposed, illustrative, or unavailable. Record source locations,
   dates, versions, and limitations. Prior chat summaries help locate evidence but do not
   establish research results. Sufficient supplied aggregates may be reported as supplied;
   absence of raw data limits recalculation, not all useful writing.
3. Audit gaps before expanding: section/location | missing element | consequence |
   evidence needed | proposed repair | priority. Distinguish absent evidence from poor
   explanation and genuine contradiction. Correct clarity immediately; ask only for
   missing information that prevents a truthful substantive claim.
4. Expand with evidence, explanation of mechanisms, justified choices, comparisons,
   boundary conditions, and implications. Do not pad with repetitive background,
   unsupported claims, invented interviews, statistics, tests, citations, or features.
5. Deliver revised prose plus a separate concise gap/change note. Keep editorial notes,
   audit tables, and requests for missing sources outside manuscript prose unless requested.
   Missing evidence should not cause supported paragraphs to remain empty headings.
6. Before saving, check objectives, terminology, population counts, measures, units,
   standard editions, figure references, and scope across chapters. Report contradictions
   without silently changing other chapters. For live edits use the document connector
   skill, revision guards, scoped mutations, and readback. Preserve native tables, figures,
   citation fields, and unrelated formatting. Inspect exported pages when available;
   report export denial as a layout-verification limitation, not a failed content edit.

## ISO reference policy

Verify applicable editions in official catalogues when executing a standards-dependent
task. Preserve the edition adopted in the approved method; upgrading the document date
does not authorize recoding the instrument. Do not mix characteristic sets silently.

The 2011 model distinguishes product quality and quality in use: usability belongs to
product quality and satisfaction belongs to quality in use. Document any local scale's
operationalization instead of asserting that local labels reproduce the whole standard.
Catalogue summaries identify scope; use accessible standard text for exact clauses and
formal conformance assessment. Never invent clause numbers. Say “informed by” or
“mapped to selected characteristics” when that is the evidence available. These skill
workflows are practical research guidance, not verbatim ISO procedures.

## Restrictions and phrasing

- Do not fabricate references, quotations, DOI links, participant consent, ethics approval,
  random selection, test results, interview findings, thresholds, or security/legal compliance.
- Distinguish research design (e.g. developmental-evaluative) from development process
  (e.g. Agile-Scrum). Neither automatically establishes experimental causality.
- Use past tense for completed procedures supported by records, future tense for proposed
  work, and present tense for established descriptions. Do not change planned work into
  completed work merely to make a final manuscript sound finished.
- Replace vague praise with actor, action, condition, evidence, and boundary. Example:
  “The system guarantees efficiency” -> “The platform centralizes submission records;
  its effect on processing time requires a comparison under defined conditions.”
- Replace unsupported novelty with a scoped research gap. “No system exists” ->
  “The reviewed systems do not document [verified capability] within [reviewed context].”
- Replace unsupported significance with descriptive wording. “Significantly improved”
  requires an appropriate analysis; “respondents reported favorable usability” does not.
- Use the manuscript's citation style. Attribute claims to the actual source, distinguish
  paraphrase from quotation, and do not treat vendor descriptions as independent evaluation.
- Expanding content never authorizes increasing sample sizes, adding empirical claims,
  copying copyrighted passages, changing objectives after results, or disguising limitations.

## CMS-V2 consistency

Use Phase 0–4 for workflow stages and Capstone 1–3 for separate semester courses.
Chapters 1–3 belong to proposal work; Chapters 4–5 belong to final manuscript work.
Primary roles are student, instructor, faculty; adviser/secretary/panelist are faculty
appointments. Instructors are excluded from committee appointments. A committee has
one Adviser, one Secretary, and three distinct Panelists with role-conflict restrictions.
Secretary endorsement precedes the Phase 4 ADM signature tiers. Repository rules define
intended behavior; they do not prove the behavior was tested in a study.

## Cross-chapter handoff and acceptance checklist

Chapter 1 objective -> Chapter 2 rationale/gap -> Chapter 3 method and measure ->
Chapter 4 evidence and interpretation -> Chapter 5 conclusion and recommendation.

Check that every objective has a method and a result status, every factual addition has
a source, and every unresolved gap has a specific next step. Numerical values, sample
counts, and standard editions must agree or be explicitly flagged. New sections need
substantive paragraphs, not headings alone. Do not claim all checks passed when the
source, rendering, or independent evaluation remains unavailable.
