## Findings

The pilot involved three participants, each completing two equally weighted, positively scored usability items. Following the approved analysis, participant composites were 2.00 for A, 3.00 for B, and 4.00 for C. Their mean was 3.00 and their sample standard deviation was 1.00. These results describe the supplied usability responses under the study’s adopted ISO/IEC 25010:2011 framework. The six item ratings are observations nested within three participants, not six independent respondents. No interpretation bands, satisfaction measures, or technical test results were supplied.

## Conclusion

The pilot provides a descriptive summary of perceived usability for three participants, with a mean composite score of 3.00 (sample SD 1.00). Without approved interpretation bands, the result cannot be assigned an Excellent classification. The evidence does not evaluate satisfaction or demonstrate technical quality or ISO compliance. The manuscript should retain ISO/IEC 25010:2011 to remain consistent with its methods; relabeling the existing evaluation as the nine-characteristic 2023 model would change its stated methodological basis without supporting evidence.

## Recommendation

The research team should retain participant-level composite scoring and report n=3, verifying that tables and prose reproduce the calculations below. Before a future evaluation, the team should document interpretation criteria and any additional constructs or technical measures required by its objectives. Any proposed adoption of another ISO edition should receive an explicit methods and instrument review rather than a retrospective label change. Verify future conclusions against the measures actually collected and the criteria declared for that evaluation.

**Separate calculation note:** All supplied values are complete, positively scored, and equally weighted; no missing-value or reverse-scoring adjustment is required.

- Participant means: A=(1+3)/2=2; B=(3+3)/2=3; C=(5+3)/2=4.

- Sample SD: sqrt(((2−3)²+(3−3)²+(4−3)²)/(3−1))=sqrt(2/2)=1.
- Item sample SDs are 2 and 0. Their average happens to be 1 in this particular dataset, matching the composite SD numerically. That coincidence does not justify averaging item SDs: the approved quantity is calculated from participant composites, and item covariance generally affects it.
