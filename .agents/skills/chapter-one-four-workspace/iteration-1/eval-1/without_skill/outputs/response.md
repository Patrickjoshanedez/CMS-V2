## Chapter 1: Problem and objectives

The supplied institutional workflow note identifies email and paper as submission channels. This establishes the submission setting, but does not establish the frequency of lost files, review delays, duplicate submissions, or version confusion. These possible difficulties require local investigation before they can be presented as observed institutional problems. The study therefore addresses how a capstone submission system can support the documented workflow and how its suitability can be evaluated against explicit requirements.

The general objective is to develop and evaluate a submission system informed by the institution's existing process. Proposed specific objectives are: (1) document submission steps, responsible users, and information requirements through an approved requirements-gathering procedure; (2) implement submission and version-tracking functions traceable to the validated requirements; and (3) assess the implemented functions using predefined test cases and collect user feedback using a documented instrument. Each objective should map respectively to a requirements record, a feature-to-requirement matrix, and evaluation evidence. A speed comparison would require a separate baseline, consistent task definitions, and recorded completion times; no percentage improvement is currently established.

## Chapter 2: Synthesis and gap

Study A's supplied abstract describes version tracking in one university. Study B's supplied abstract describes similarity matching. Together, they identify relevant capabilities, but the supplied abstracts provide neither demonstrated workflow outcomes nor matching error rates. Their effectiveness cannot be inferred from these descriptions alone.

The defensible gap concerns evidence of fit and evaluated performance within the documented local workflow. It does not establish that the proposed system is the first of its kind. Full bibliographic details, complete studies, and broader literature coverage remain necessary before final citations and a stronger research-gap claim can be written. The claimed guaranteed 80% improvement is unsupported and has been removed.
