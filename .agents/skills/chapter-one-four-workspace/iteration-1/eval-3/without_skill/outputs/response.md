## Chapter 4: Findings and discussion

The supplied summary reports usability with a mean of 4.53 and a standard deviation of 0.30, and satisfaction with a mean of 4.48 and a standard deviation of 0.34, among a reported 77 respondents. Both means fall within the approved “Excellent” interpretation band of 4.21–5.00. On this basis, the reported aggregate ratings indicate favorable perceptions of usability and satisfaction among the respondents represented in the summary.

The usability mean exceeds the satisfaction mean by 0.05 points. This is a descriptive difference; its statistical significance and practical importance have not been established. Likewise, the reported standard deviations summarize dispersion but cannot establish that every participant rated the system favorably. Interpretation is limited by the absence of raw responses and information about aggregation and missing-data handling.

The evaluation retains the instrument's adopted ISO/IEC 25010:2011 framework. Favorable questionnaire ratings alone do not demonstrate full compliance with that framework or verify all system characteristics. The findings describe reported perceptions rather than measured improvement over an earlier process. Without a baseline or a suitable comparison design, “significant improvement” is not supported. The illustrative 200 ms benchmark is not an observed system result and cannot substantiate a performance conclusion.

## Evidence gaps

The reported respondent count, item-level data, scoring procedure, and calculation of the aggregate standard deviations require verification. Recruitment records and response completeness are also needed to clarify who the results represent. Any claims about operational performance require executed test records, defined workloads, measurement conditions, and observed timings. These missing materials limit verification and generalization; they do not erase the supplied descriptive results or justify inventing additional findings.
