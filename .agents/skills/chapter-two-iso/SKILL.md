# Chapter Two: Literature, Related Systems, and Synthesis

Read [the shared chapter guidance](../chapter-one-four-common.md) first. The
institutional template governs chapter organization and citation style. ISO quality
models may organize comparison criteria; they do not prescribe a literature review.

## Establish the review question and source boundary

Read Chapter 1 objectives and scope, the current literature review, supplied source
files, and the bibliography. Identify whether the user requests editing existing
sources, expanding through research, or conducting a new review. Preserve valid
citations and distinguish verified source text from titles, abstracts, and metadata.

Build a source ledger with author, year, exact title, publication venue, DOI or stable
URL, retrieved section/pages, study context, method, sample, findings, and limitations.
Check that each citation supports the adjacent claim. A search result or title does
not establish the paper's detailed findings. If only an abstract is available, use
only what it supports and disclose the access limit in the separate audit note.

## What to include

| Component | Content and purpose | Common gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Review introduction | Scope, themes, and relation to the research objectives | Unexplained collection of sources |
| Conceptual literature | Definitions and concepts needed to understand the study | Dictionary definitions without application |
| Empirical related studies | Methods, context, findings, and limitations relevant to the problem | Author-by-author summaries with no comparison |
| Related systems | Verified capabilities, intended users, scope, and evaluation evidence | Vendor feature claims treated as proven effectiveness |
| Standards or quality framework | Applicable standard, edition, role, and scope of use | ISO name-dropping or mixing editions |
| Thematic synthesis | Agreements, disagreements, contextual differences, and implications | Repetition of abstracts |
| Research gap and contribution | What the reviewed evidence leaves unresolved and what this study addresses | Unsupported claims that no previous system exists |

Separate related literature and related studies only when the approved template
requires it. Choose themes that answer the study's questions, such as workflow
coordination, archival access, similarity analysis, and evaluation of user experience.
Local/foreign labels are not a substitute for a useful conceptual synthesis.

## Search and citation restrictions

When expanding, record databases/sites, search terms, date range, search date, and
selection rationale. Seek relevant primary studies and authoritative documentation.
Balance foundational sources with recent evidence; do not discard a relevant work
solely because it is older. Include contrary findings and distinguish comparable
from non-comparable populations, methods, datasets, and outcome measures.

Do not call a narrative review systematic unless a reproducible protocol, selection
criteria, screening process, and appropriate reporting evidence exist. Do not invent
search counts, exclusion reasons, DOIs, quotations, page numbers, or study results.
Do not turn absent information into a negative finding: "not reported" differs from
"not supported" or "not implemented". Preserve proper attribution when paraphrasing;
changing words does not remove the need to cite the source.

## ISO-informed use

Use the adopted ISO/IEC quality-model edition to define comparison dimensions only
where the source supports the mapping. Label an author's assessment as that author's
assessment; a product's features do not establish ISO certification or conformity.
Keep ISO/IEC 25010:2011 and 25010:2023 constructs distinct, and do not silently move
quality-in-use concepts into the newer product model.

[ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018](https://www.iso.org/standard/72089.html) can inform how
literature-derived stakeholder needs become traceable requirements.
[ISO/IEC/IEEE 15289:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/74909.html) concerns lifecycle
documentation information; it is not a thesis formatting standard. Verify exact
editions and clauses from accessible authoritative material before detailed claims.

## Identify and expand gaps

Use a working evidence matrix: source -> theme -> method/context -> supported
finding -> limitation -> implication for the present objective. Then identify:

- **Coverage gaps:** an objective has no relevant literature or only product marketing.
- **Synthesis gaps:** sources are listed without explaining agreement or disagreement.
- **Evidence gaps:** a citation does not support a claim or full text is unavailable.
- **Transferability gaps:** findings from a different context are applied without justification.
- **Contribution gaps:** the proposed contribution does not address the stated problem.

For each gap, provide its location, consequence, evidence needed, and repair. Expand
using supported comparisons, contextual explanations, and implications. Avoid padding
with additional citations that do not change the argument. When evidence is missing,
write a bounded synthesis and list the research need separately rather than fabricate.

## Phrasing patterns

| Avoid | Prefer when supported |
| --- | --- |
| "All studies prove automated systems are better." | "The reviewed studies report benefits under their respective evaluation conditions; differences in methods limit direct comparison." |
| "No system has this feature." | "The sources reviewed here do not establish how this feature is implemented in the target institutional context." |
| "The authors used AI, so detection was accurate." | "The authors used an AI-based method; its reported performance should be interpreted with the dataset, metric, and evaluation conditions." |
| "This research is the first." | "This study examines the identified workflow and evaluation needs in the specified setting." |

Structure synthesis paragraphs as theme claim -> compared evidence -> explanation
of differences -> implication. Use present tense for continuing concepts and past
tense for completed study procedures or findings. Do not claim contradictions when
studies measured different constructs.

## Delivery and verification

Use [the chapter template](assets/chapter-template.md). Deliver revised prose,
verified citations in the existing style, and a separate gap/source-verification report.
Keep search logs and editorial notes outside the manuscript unless requested as an
appendix. Check bidirectional citation-bibliography consistency, claim support,
objective coverage, edition consistency, and whether the gap follows from the review.
For live edits, preserve unrelated content and follow the relevant document skill.
