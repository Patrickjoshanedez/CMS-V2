# Chapter One: Problem, Purpose, and Boundaries

Read [the shared chapter guidance](../chapter-one-four-common.md) before applying
this skill. Use the approved institutional template for section names and order;
ISO does not prescribe thesis chapter numbering or a universal introduction format.

## Inputs and task boundaries

Inspect the existing chapter, approved title, institutional template, stakeholder
evidence, objectives, and current project scope. Read relevant Chapter 3 methods
and Chapter 4 findings when revising a completed study. A writing request alone
does not authorize changing research commitments or inventing interviews.

Confirm whether the study is proposed, ongoing, or completed. Keep tense consistent
with the actual status; describe proposed capabilities as intended capabilities.
Reuse available sources before asking for missing information. A usable partial
draft is preferable to either unsupported completion or withholding supported prose.

## What to include

| Content | What it should establish | Evidence or check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Background | Relevant domain context, local workflow, affected stakeholders, and reason for investigation | Current primary literature and documented local observations |
| Problem statement | Specific shortcomings, consequences, and the bounded problem being addressed | Records, interviews, policies, or clearly attributed observations |
| General objective | The study's overarching intended contribution | Consistency with title, design, and scope |
| Specific objectives | Distinct, observable outputs or evaluation questions | Each maps to a method and an eventual result |
| Significance | Who could benefit, how, and under what conditions | Separate plausible benefits from demonstrated improvements |
| Scope and delimitations | Included users, workflows, data, platform, setting, period, and deliberate exclusions | Approved requirements and feasible methods |
| Limitations | Constraints affecting validity or interpretation | Known design/data constraints, not a list of features omitted by choice |
| Operational definitions | Terms with a study-specific meaning | Define how a term is used or measured here |

Include a framework only if required by the institutional template or research
design. Do not force a decorative input-process-output diagram into every study.

## ISO-informed patterns and restrictions

Use [ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018](https://www.iso.org/standard/72089.html) as a
requirements-engineering reference when connecting stakeholder needs to explicit,
verifiable requirements. Requirements and research objectives are related but
different: an objective describes a research activity or outcome, while a system
requirement describes required behavior or a constraint.

Use [ISO/IEC/IEEE 15289:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/74909.html) as a
documentation reference when planning traceable information items. Neither
reference establishes that a manuscript or implemented product is ISO-certified.
Public catalogue summaries do not support invented clause numbers or full
conformance assertions. Check the adopted edition before any detailed claim.

Create a working matrix: problem evidence -> stakeholder need -> objective ->
planned method -> expected evidence -> scope boundary. Mark missing links rather
than filling them with assumptions. Keep this matrix outside the chapter unless
the template calls for it.

## Find what is lacking

1. Identify a missing local context, unsupported problem frequency, vague objective,
   unexplained stakeholder benefit, scope ambiguity, or undefined operational term.
2. Classify each gap as **blocking**, **important**, or **editorial**. A missing
   measurement method is more consequential than a weak transition sentence.
3. State the exact location, why it matters, needed evidence, and a proposed repair.
4. Distinguish missing prose from missing research. Existing evidence can support
   expansion; absent data require collection or a bounded claim, not more words.

## Expand and phrase carefully

Expand a paragraph through context -> evidence -> consequence -> study response.
Add an explanation of a supported mechanism or boundary, not repeated claims
that technology is important. Use one main idea per paragraph and transitions
that show the logical relationship to the next paragraph.

| Weak or overstated | Safer pattern |
| --- | --- |
| "Manual processing is always inefficient." | "The documented workflow requires separate submission and review records, creating a need to examine coordination difficulties." |
| "The system eliminates plagiarism." | "The system is intended to support academic review by identifying potentially similar text." |
| "To make an effective system." | "To evaluate the system's task completion and perceived usability using the specified test and survey procedures." |
| "The system will save time." | "The platform may reduce repeated administrative steps; time savings require comparison with the existing process." |

Examples illustrate claim boundaries, not facts to paste into an unrelated study.
Do not add numerical targets, sample sizes, respondents, or statistical tests unless
approved or explicitly identified as proposals awaiting a methodological decision.

## CMS-V2 checks

Keep primary roles separate from faculty committee appointments. Describe Phase
0-4 separately from Capstone courses 1-3. The course instructor cannot fill an
Adviser, Secretary, or Panelist committee appointment. State these as requirements
unless their implementation has been verified; policy alone is not test evidence.

## Deliver and verify

Use [the drafting template](assets/chapter-template.md). Deliver substantive revised
prose, then a separate gap report with evidence needed and unresolved decisions.
Keep editorial notes out of the manuscript. Verify objective-method traceability,
source attribution, tense, scope consistency, and the absence of unsupported
effectiveness claims. Preserve unaffected document sections during live edits and
use the relevant document-editing skill for readback and layout verification.
